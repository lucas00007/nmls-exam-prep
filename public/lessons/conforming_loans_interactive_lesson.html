Case Briefing START HERE

Conforming loans are conventional mortgages that meet Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac purchase guidelines. Understanding the secondary market, loan limits, underwriting standards, and PMI rules is essential for the general mortgage knowledge section of the exam.

EXHIBIT A
What Is a Conforming Loan?
A conventional loan that meets BOTH Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac guidelines: (1) loan amount at or below FHFA conforming limit, AND (2) underwriting meets GSE standards. All conforming loans are conventional — but not all conventional loans are conforming.
Foundation
Exam trap: Jumbo loans are conventional but NOT conforming. A conventional loan simply means not government-backed (not FHA/VA/USDA). A conforming loan means it also meets the loan limit and underwriting standards for GSE purchase.

EXHIBIT B
2024 Conforming Limits
Standard 1-unit: $766,550. High-cost areas: $1,149,825. The limit applies to the LOAN AMOUNT — not the purchase price. FHFA sets limits annually based on home price changes.
Memorize these
Exam trap: The limit applies to the LOAN AMOUNT — not the purchase price. A $900,000 home with 20% down = $720,000 loan → conforming. A $700,000 home with 5% down = $665,000 loan → also conforming. Calculate the loan amount, not the price.

EXHIBIT C
PMI Rules — 80/78
PMI required when LTV > 80%. Borrower can REQUEST cancellation at 80% LTV. PMI automatically cancels at 78% LTV. Both based on ORIGINAL value and ORIGINAL amortization schedule — not current appraised value.
80% vs 78%
Exam trap — most tested PMI fact: Request at 80%, automatic cancel at 78%. Two different events, two different percentages. BOTH based on the ORIGINAL purchase price and ORIGINAL amortization schedule — not the current appraised value or extra payments made.

FILE 001
Fannie Mae vs. Freddie Mac
Fannie Mae (FNMA): AUS = Desktop Underwriter (DU), 3% down program = HomeReady. Freddie Mac (FHLMC): AUS = Loan Product Advisor (LPA), 3% down program = Home Possible.
Know both GSEs
Exam trap: DU = Fannie Mae. LPA = Freddie Mac. These are tested constantly. Both GSEs are regulated by FHFA and placed in conservatorship in 2008. Neither makes loans directly — they buy loans from lenders and package them as MBS.

FILE 002
Primary vs. Secondary Market
Primary market = where loans are ORIGINATED between lenders and borrowers. Secondary market = where loans are BOUGHT and SOLD (Fannie/Freddie operate here). MLOs work in the primary market.
Two markets
Exam trap: Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac operate in the SECONDARY market — they do NOT lend directly to borrowers. MLOs originate loans in the PRIMARY market. When asked where Fannie operates: secondary market. Where MLOs originate: primary market.

FILE 003
Standard Underwriting Guidelines
Min credit score: 620. Max back-end DTI: 45% standard (50% with strong AUS). Max LTV: 97% for HomeReady/Home Possible (3% down). PMI required when LTV > 80%.
Key standards
Credit score tiers: 760+ = best pricing. Each tier down increases the loan level price adjustment (LLPA). 620 is the floor — below 620, conventional conforming is generally unavailable. Higher score = lower rate in the secondary market pricing grid.

FILE 004
Self-Employed Income
Borrowers owning 25%+ of a business are treated as self-employed for qualifying. Requires 2 years of personal + business tax returns. Income = 2-year average of net income from returns.
25% threshold
Exam trap: The threshold is 25% ownership — not 51%, not 50%, not majority. A 26% owner must provide full self-employment documentation. A 24% owner does not — they are treated as an employee of the business.

FILE 005
Rental Income Rules
Only 75% of gross monthly rent is used to offset the rental property's PITI payment. The 25% haircut accounts for vacancies and expenses. Net rental income (or loss) affects qualifying DTI.
75% of gross rent
Calculation: Gross rent $2,000/month × 75% = $1,500 usable income. If rental PITI is $1,400, net positive income = $100/month. If rental PITI is $1,700, net loss = -$200/month counted as a monthly obligation.

FILE 006
FHA MIP vs PMI
PMI (conventional): cancellable at 78% LTV per HPA. FHA MIP: if original LTV > 90% → lasts LIFE OF LOAN. Only way to eliminate FHA MIP on most loans = refinance into conventional.
Key difference
Exam trap: FHA MIP does NOT cancel automatically like PMI. If the original LTV was above 90% at FHA origination, annual MIP lasts the entire loan term. To eliminate it, the borrower must refinance into a conventional loan once they have 20% equity.

FILE 007
Down Payment Programs
HomeReady (Fannie): 3% down, income ≤ 80% AMI, non-borrower household income counts. Home Possible (Freddie): 3% down, income ≤ 80% AMI. Both require homebuyer education for first-time buyers.
3% down options
Key requirement: BOTH HomeReady and Home Possible require the borrower's income to be at or below 80% of Area Median Income (AMI). No income limit applies to the standard 5% down conventional loan — income limits are specific to these 3% programs.

FILE 008
Non-Conforming Loans
Non-conforming = exceeds loan limit (jumbo) OR doesn't meet GSE underwriting. Cannot be sold to Fannie/Freddie. Lenders hold in portfolio or sell to private secondary market. Stricter requirements.
Jumbo loans
Jumbo loan characteristics: Typically requires 720+ credit score, 10-20% down, 12+ months reserves, stricter DTI requirements. Higher rate (usually) than conforming. No GSE purchase guarantee — lender bears full credit risk unless sold to private investor.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

  • Conforming = loan amount ≤ limit AND meets GSE guidelines
  • Limit applies to loan amount — not purchase price
  • DU = Fannie Mae · LPA = Freddie Mac
  • PMI: request at 80%, auto-cancel at 78% (original values)
  • Self-employed = 25%+ ownership — requires tax returns
  • FHA MIP with LTV > 90% lasts life of loan

EXAM TRAPS

  • Purchase price vs. loan amount — limit tests loan amount
  • PMI 80% vs 78% — two different events
  • DU vs LPA — don't mix up the two GSEs
  • FHA MIP does NOT cancel like PMI
  • 75% rental income — not 100%
  • HomeReady/Home Possible have income limits — 5% down does not
