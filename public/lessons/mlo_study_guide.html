Pre-Licensing Hours Breakdown
  • 3 hours — Federal mortgage law
  • 3 hours — Ethics (fraud, consumer protection, fair lending)
  • 2 hours — Non-traditional mortgage products
  • 12 hours — Electives (state may add more)
  • Total: 20 hours minimum
Annual CE Hours Breakdown
  • 3 hours — Federal law
  • 2 hours — Ethics
  • 2 hours — Non-traditional products
  • 1 hour — Elective
  • Total: 8 hours minimum per year
  • Cannot repeat same course in consecutive years
Who Needs an MLO License
  • State-licensed: non-bank MLOs must be fully licensed
  • Federally registered: bank/CU employees register only (no state license)
  • Bank subsidiaries: must get state license (not exempt)
  • Clerical/admin only: exempt from licensing
  • Temporary authority: can originate while new state app pending
Secondary Market Players
  • Fannie Mae (FNMA) — buys conventional conforming loans
  • Freddie Mac (FHLMC) — buys conventional conforming loans
  • Ginnie Mae (GNMA) — guarantees MBS backed by FHA/VA/USDA loans; government agency
  • Fannie & Freddie = GSEs (not government agencies)
  • Securitization frees up capital for new loans
NMLS License Facts
  • Unique Identifier: public-facing ID on all loan docs
  • Consumer Access: free public database to verify MLOs
  • Felony involving fraud/dishonesty within 7 years = denial
  • Financial crimes = permanent bar
  • Renew by December 31 each year
  • Sponsorship required — cannot originate without active sponsor
Lender Types
  • Retail lender — funds own loans, sells to secondary market
  • Mortgage broker — originates, never funds; uses wholesale lenders
  • Correspondent lender — funds in own name, then sells
  • Portfolio lender — keeps loans on own books
  • Wholesale lender — funds broker-originated loans
  • Warehouse line = short-term funding until loan is sold
⚡ Exam Trap: Bank employees REGISTER with NMLS — they do NOT get a state license. Bank SUBSIDIARIES must get a state license. This distinction appears frequently in scenario questions.

ECOA vs Fair Housing Act — Protected Classes
  • Both laws: race, color, religion, national origin, sex
  • ECOA only: marital status, age, receipt of public assistance
  • FHA only: familial status, disability
  • ECOA = 8 classes total | FHA = 7 classes total
  • Enforced by: ECOA→CFPB | FHA→HUD and DOJ
TRID Tolerance Categories
  • Zero tolerance (cannot increase): lender fees, transfer taxes, required lender-selected services
  • 10% aggregate tolerance: recording fees, required borrower-selected from list
  • Unlimited tolerance: prepaids, escrow deposits, optional owner's title, borrower-chosen providers NOT on list
  • Cure = refund excess within 3 calendar years of closing
3 Events That Reset CD 3-Day Clock
  • APR increases more than 1/8% (0.125%) for fixed
  • APR increases more than 1/4% (0.25%) for variable
  • Loan product changes (e.g., fixed → ARM)
  • Prepayment penalty is added
  • All other changes do NOT restart the clock
RESPA Section 8 — Key Rules
  • No kickbacks or referral fees among settlement providers
  • No de minimis exception — any thing of value = violation
  • Fee splits OK only if BOTH parties perform actual services
  • ABA: must disclose + consumer must have choice + return on ownership only
  • Section 9: seller cannot require specific title company
  • Civil penalty = 3x the charge + criminal: $10K fine / 1 yr jail
Right of Rescission (TILA)
  • Applies to: refinances, HELOCs on PRIMARY residence
  • Does NOT apply to: purchase money mortgages
  • 3 business days = all days except Sundays + federal holidays
  • If rescission rights not properly disclosed: extended to 3 YEARS
  • Lender must return ALL finance charges upon valid rescission
TRID Exemptions
  • Reverse mortgages (HECMs) — use HUD disclosures
  • HELOCs — use separate Reg Z disclosures
  • Mobile home loans not secured by real property
  • Certain no-interest loans
  • Commercial/business purpose loans
⚡ Most Tested Scenario: An MLO receives something of value from a title company. Always ask: Was it for an actual service performed? If YES → potentially legal. If it's for referrals → RESPA Section 8 violation. Amount does NOT matter.
⚡ ECOA Timing: Adverse action notice must be sent within 30 days of a COMPLETED application. Must state SPECIFIC reasons — vague reasons are a violation.

TOPIC 03

Loan Origination & Application Process

Exam Weight: 25% — Single highest weighted topic
6Application trigger elements
3Business days — LE delivery
10Business days — LE expiration
3%QM points & fees cap
43%Max DTI for manual QM
6 Application Trigger Elements (TRID)
  • 1. Borrower's name
  • 2. Social Security number
  • 3. Income
  • 4. Property address
  • 5. Estimated property value
  • 6. Desired loan amount
  • All 6 present → Loan Estimate required within 3 business days
Pre-Qual vs Pre-Approval
  • Pre-qualification: unverified, self-reported info only
  • Pre-approval: verified docs + credit report pulled
  • Neither is a binding commitment to lend
  • Neither triggers TRID (no property address yet)
  • Pre-approval expires in 60–90 days
  • Pre-approval = stronger signal to sellers in competitive markets
DTI Calculations
  • Front-end (housing) DTI = PITI ÷ Gross Monthly Income
  • Back-end (total) DTI = (PITI + ALL debts) ÷ GMI
  • Uses GROSS (pre-tax) income — never net
  • Includes: credit cards, car, student loans, alimony, child support
  • Excludes: utilities, phone, food, 401(k) loan repayments (conventional)
  • HOA dues: included in front-end DTI
  • Deferred student loans: use 1% of balance if no payment stated
LO Compensation Rule (Reg Z)
  • MLO cannot be paid by BOTH borrower AND lender on same loan
  • Cannot be compensated based on loan TERMS (rate, fees, LTV)
  • CAN be paid based on loan volume or fixed amounts
  • Prevents steering borrowers to higher-cost products for higher MLO pay
Income Documentation
  • W-2 employee: 2 years W-2s + 30 days pay stubs + VOE
  • Self-employed (25%+ ownership): 2 years personal + business tax returns
  • Commission/bonus: 2-year history required
  • Part-time: 2-year history required
  • Social Security: can gross up by 25% (÷ 0.75)
  • Rental income: 75% of gross rents (25% vacancy factor)
  • Self-employed: add back depreciation to Schedule C net income
Rate Lock & Intent to Proceed
  • Intent to proceed required before collecting most fees
  • Credit report fee: only fee collectible BEFORE intent to proceed
  • Rate lock = lender guarantees rate for set period (30–60 days typical)
  • Changed circumstance: allows revised LE with updated fees
  • Lender errors are NOT valid changed circumstances
⚡ Exam Trap: The exam loves testing what IS and IS NOT in the DTI. Remember: utilities, subscriptions, and living expenses are NEVER in DTI. Only credit obligations with required monthly payments count.

ARM Structure — 2/2/5 Cap
  • First 2 = initial adjustment cap (1st change after fixed period)
  • Second 2 = periodic cap (each subsequent adjustment)
  • 5 = lifetime cap (max total change from start rate)
  • Rate = Index + Margin (margin is FIXED; index floats)
  • Qualify at: HIGHER of note rate or fully indexed rate
  • SOFR replaced LIBOR as dominant ARM index
FHA Key Facts
  • MIP: 1.75% upfront (can be financed) + annual monthly premium
  • MIP for life of loan if LTV > 90% at origination
  • MIP removed at 11 years if 10%+ down at origination
  • Max seller concessions: 6% of purchase price
  • 203(k): purchase + renovation in one loan
  • Streamline refinance: no appraisal, minimal docs, must net tangible benefit
VA Key Facts
  • No down payment, no PMI, no minimum credit score (VA)
  • Funding fee: ~2.15% first use / ~3.3% subsequent use (0% down)
  • Disabled veterans (10%+ rating): EXEMPT from funding fee
  • IRRRL (streamline refi): minimal docs, no appraisal typically
  • Entitlement can be restored and reused after payoff
  • VA loans are assumable
USDA Key Facts
  • 100% financing — no down payment
  • Property must be in USDA-eligible rural/suburban area
  • Income limit: typically 115% of area median income
  • Section 502 Guaranteed: most common program
  • Waiting period after Ch. 7: 3 years (longer than FHA's 2 years)
Conventional PMI Rules
  • Required when LTV > 80% (down payment less than 20%)
  • Homeowners Protection Act: auto-cancel at 78% LTV
  • Borrower can request cancellation at 80% LTV
  • Piggyback (80-10-10): avoids PMI by keeping 1st mortgage at 80%
  • PMI protects the LENDER — not the borrower
Qualified Mortgage (QM) Rules
  • Points and fees cap: 3% of loan amount ($100K+)
  • No negative amortization
  • No balloon payments (most cases)
  • No interest-only payments
  • Max loan term: 30 years
  • Manual underwrite QM: max 43% DTI
  • DU/AUS approval: up to 45–50% DTI
⚡ Key Distinction: USDA and VA both offer 0% down — but USDA is for rural areas with income limits; VA is for eligible veterans with no income limit or geographic restriction. Know which is which in scenarios.

The Four C's of Credit
  • Credit — payment history, score, derogatory marks
  • Capacity — income, employment, DTI ratios
  • Capital — assets, down payment, reserves
  • Collateral — property value, condition, type
  • Underwriter makes final approve/suspend/deny decision — NOT the MLO
Credit Score Rules
  • Tri-merge: pull all 3 bureaus; use the middle score
  • Two borrowers: use lower of the two middle scores
  • FHA 3.5% down: 580 minimum
  • FHA 10% down: 500 minimum
  • Conventional: typically 620 minimum
  • VA/USDA: no official minimum (lender overlays apply)
Reserves
  • Reserves = liquid assets REMAINING after closing
  • Measured in months of PITI
  • Seasoned funds: in account at least 60 days
  • Non-vested stock options: NOT acceptable reserves
  • 401(k) vested: acceptable at 60% of value (penalty haircut)
  • Gift funds: cannot be used as reserves (must be borrower's own)
AUS Systems
  • DU (Desktop Underwriter) = Fannie Mae's AUS
  • LPA (Loan Product Advisor) = Freddie Mac's AUS
  • TOTAL Scorecard = FHA's AUS
  • AUS findings: Approve/Eligible, Refer, Refer with Caution
  • "Refer" = file goes to manual underwriting
  • DU max DTI: 45% (50% with strong compensating factors)
Extenuating Circumstances
  • Must be: nonrecurring + beyond borrower's control
  • Qualifies: serious illness, layoff, death of wage earner, natural disaster
  • Does NOT qualify: quitting job, lifestyle choice, poor money management
  • With extenuating circumstances: waiting periods reduced (see table above)
  • Must document with letter of explanation + supporting evidence
Key Income Rules
  • All DTI uses GROSS monthly income
  • Social Security gross-up: divide by 0.75 (+25%)
  • Self-employed: 2-year average from tax returns; add back depreciation
  • Rental income: 75% of gross (25% vacancy)
  • 4506-C: IRS transcript verification — key fraud prevention tool
  • Mortgage included in BK + later foreclosure: wait from LATER event
⚡ Exam Trap: Waiting periods start from the DISCHARGE date (Ch. 7) or DISCHARGE/DISMISSAL date (Ch. 13) — NOT the filing date. Conventional foreclosure = 7 years. This is the longest waiting period in all of mortgage lending.

TOPIC 06

Appraisal & Property Valuation

Exam Weight: ~8% — Know the three approaches and independence rules
Three Appraisal Approaches
  • Sales Comparison — primary for residential (1-4 family)
  • Cost Approach — used for new construction, special-use properties
  • Income Approach — used for investment/income-producing properties
  • Appraiser RECONCILES the approaches — does NOT average them
  • Value = NOI ÷ Cap Rate (Income Approach)
Appraiser Independence
  • HVCC + Dodd-Frank: lenders cannot pressure appraisers
  • AMC (Appraisal Management Company): manages independence
  • MLOs cannot select, hire, or communicate with appraiser about value
  • Collateral Underwriter (CU): Fannie Mae's appraisal risk tool
  • ROV (Reconsideration of Value): formal request with missed comps
  • Contingent fee (paying based on value reached) = USPAP violation
Key Appraisal Facts
  • USPAP: ethical standards all appraisers must follow
  • Preferred comps: within 6 months and 1 mile
  • Appraisal valid for: 12 months maximum
  • ECOA: borrower must receive appraisal copy promptly — no later than 3 days before closing
  • GLA: above-grade, heated/cooled finished square footage only
  • Subject property = the property being appraised
Types of Depreciation
  • Physical deterioration — wear and tear, deferred maintenance
  • Functional obsolescence — outdated features (1 bath in 4BR home)
  • External/economic obsolescence — nearby landfill, highway noise
  • As-is value = current condition
  • As-repaired/subject-to = value after specified repairs complete
⚡ Key Distinction: An appraisal estimates market value for the LENDER'S benefit. A home inspection examines physical condition for the BUYER'S benefit. They serve completely different purposes and neither replaces the other.

Ethics & Fraud Prevention

Exam Weight: 16% — Scenario-heavy; know fraud types cold
30Days — SAR filing deadline
60Days — SAR if suspect ID needed
7Years — felony bar (fraud/dishonesty)
∞Permanent bar — financial crimes
Fraud for Profit vs Fraud for Housing
  • Fraud for profit = industry insiders (MLO, appraiser, title) extracting money
  • Fraud for housing = borrower misrepresenting to get a loan they don't qualify for
  • Both are federal crimes
  • Fraud for profit is more heavily prosecuted
Common Fraud Schemes
  • Straw buyer — using someone else's credit to get a loan
  • Occupancy fraud — calling investment property a primary residence
  • Chunking — multiple investors, multiple simultaneous loans, hidden from lenders
  • Equity stripping / loan flipping — repeated refis to extract equity via fees
  • Inflated appraisal — appraiser overvalues to enable larger loan
  • Double-selling — same loan sold to two different investors
  • Wire fraud — fake closing instructions redirecting funds
Fair Lending Violations
  • Redlining — refusing to lend in area based on racial composition
  • Steering — directing protected class borrowers to worse loan products
  • Disparate treatment — intentionally treating applicants differently
  • Disparate impact — neutral policy disproportionately harms protected class
  • Both disparate treatment AND impact can be illegal
BSA/AML Requirements
  • SAR (Suspicious Activity Report): file within 30 days of detecting suspicious activity
  • Extension to 60 days if time needed to identify suspect
  • File with FinCEN (Financial Crimes Enforcement Network)
  • Subject of SAR CANNOT be notified it was filed
  • USA PATRIOT Act: KYC (Know Your Customer) / CIP requirements
  • Real estate = known vehicle for money laundering
Predatory Lending Indicators
  • Excessive fees not justified by services
  • Loan flipping / churning (repeated refis)
  • Negative amortization products
  • Balloon payments targeting vulnerable borrowers
  • High-pressure sales tactics
  • Targeting elderly or minority borrowers with worse terms
GLBA Privacy (Gramm-Leach-Bliley)
  • NPI (non-public personal information) must be protected
  • Annual privacy notices required to consumers
  • Can share NPI for: loan servicing, legal compliance, service providers under agreement
  • Cannot share for marketing to unaffiliated third parties without opt-out
  • FCRA: if credit report used in adverse action → must name bureau + provide dispute rights
⚡ Ethics Exam Rule: In ANY ethics scenario, ask: Did the MLO put their interest above the borrower's? Was there undisclosed compensation? Was a borrower steered based on protected class? Yes to any = violation.

TOPIC 08

Closing & Settlement

Exam Weight: ~8% — Know documents and timing
Key Closing Documents
  • Promissory Note — borrower's promise to repay; personal obligation
  • Deed of Trust / Mortgage — security instrument; pledges property as collateral
  • Closing Disclosure — final itemized costs; 3 business days before closing
  • Title Commitment — pre-closing title insurance binder
  • Note and deed of trust are SEPARATE documents
Title Insurance
  • Owner's policy — protects buyer from past title defects
  • Lender's policy — protects lender only; required for most loans
  • Both are ONE-TIME premiums (historical risk doesn't renew annually)
  • Title search: examines public records for liens and encumbrances
  • Recordation: filing deed + mortgage with county recorder establishes public notice
  • Satisfaction/release: must be recorded within 20–30 days of payoff
Closing Costs Breakdown
  • Prepaids = prepaid interest, homeowner's insurance, initial escrow deposits
  • Cash to close = down payment + closing costs − credits − deposits paid
  • APR on CD should be HIGHER than note rate (includes fees)
  • Proration: ongoing costs split between buyer/seller by ownership date
  • Per diem interest: daily interest from closing date to end of month
Wet vs Dry Closing
  • Wet closing — funds disbursed same day as signing
  • Dry closing — documents signed but funds disbursed later
  • Escrow states (CA, OR, WA) typically use dry closings
  • Attorney states (TX, NY, FL) typically use wet closings
  • Clear to close (CTC) = underwriter's final sign-off; all conditions met
⚡ Key Distinction: The promissory note = the DEBT (I promise to repay). The deed of trust = the SECURITY (if I don't repay, you can take the property). Both signed at closing. Both are separate. Both are critical.

TOPIC 09

Texas Uniform State Content (USC)

Exam Weight: 13% — Often overlooked; high value to study
⭐ This is the area most test-takers under-prepare for. The USC component covers Texas-specific mortgage laws, state licensing requirements, and Texas foreclosure procedures. At 13% of the exam it can be the difference between passing and failing.
TDSMLTexas Dept of Savings & Mortgage Lending
21Days — TX foreclosure notice
1st TueTX foreclosure sale day (each month)
3Years — TX homestead equity loan wait after prior equity loan
Texas Regulatory Structure
  • TDSML — Texas Dept of Savings and Mortgage Lending: primary state regulator for non-bank MLOs
  • Texas Finance Commission — governs TDSML and state financial regulations
  • Texas DOB — Dept of Banking: regulates state-chartered banks
  • All TX MLO licenses administered through NMLS
  • TX mortgage company license required to operate in Texas
Texas Foreclosure Process
  • Texas uses non-judicial foreclosure (deed of trust + power of sale)
  • No court action required — faster than judicial foreclosure states
  • Notice of default + 21-day notice to borrower required
  • Foreclosure sale: first Tuesday of each month at county courthouse
  • No statutory right of redemption after foreclosure sale in TX
  • Deficiency judgment: lender may pursue borrower for shortfall
Texas Homestead Laws
  • Texas homestead = strongest property protections in the US
  • Home equity loans (Texas Section 50(a)(6)) have strict rules:
  • Max 80% CLTV on home equity loans
  • Only ONE home equity loan at a time
  • Must wait 12 days after application before closing
  • Must wait 1 year before refinancing a 50(a)(6) loan
  • 3% fee cap on home equity loans
  • 12-day right of rescission (longer than federal 3-day)
Texas-Specific Loan Rules
  • Texas does NOT allow deficiency on certain purchase money loans
  • Agriculture exemptions: some farm land loans exempt from RESPA
  • Texas veterans land board: separate state programs for TX veterans
  • Prepayment penalties: generally prohibited on residential loans in TX
  • Texas home equity line of credit: governed by TX Constitution Article XVI Section 50
TX MLO License Requirements
  • Apply through NMLS + TDSML approval
  • Background check + credit check required
  • Texas requires 3 hours of TX-specific pre-licensing education (above the 20-hour federal minimum)
  • Surety bond required for mortgage companies
  • Annual renewal by December 31
  • CE: federal 8 hours + any TX-specific additional hours
Texas Home Equity — 50(a)(6) Key Numbers
  • Maximum LTV: 80% (cannot exceed)
  • Fees cap: 3% of loan amount
  • Waiting period after application: 12 days
  • Right of rescission: 3 business days (same as federal)
  • Time between equity loans: 12 months
  • Conversion to 50(a)(4) rate/term refi: allowed after 1 year
Texas USC — Exam Scenario Triggers
  • Any scenario involving a Texas homeowner borrowing against their home equity → think 50(a)(6) rules: 80% max LTV, 3% fee cap, 12-day wait
  • Any scenario involving foreclosure in Texas → non-judicial, 21-day notice, first Tuesday sale
  • Any scenario about state licensing or regulation of non-bank MLOs in Texas → TDSML governs
  • Any scenario about a Texas veteran → check both VA program AND Texas Veterans Land Board programs
  • Any scenario about refinancing a Texas home equity loan → must wait 12 months; converts to 50(a)(4)

Master Numbers Reference — Know These Cold

High-frequency exam topics — memorize every number on this list
SAFE Act & Licensing
  • Pre-licensing: 20 hours (3+3+2+12)
  • Annual CE: 8 hours (3+2+2+1)
  • NMLS passing score: 75%

TRID / Disclosure Timelines
  • Loan Estimate delivery: within 3 business days of application
  • Closing Disclosure received: at least 3 business days before closing
  • Right of Rescission: 3 business days (refi/HELOC on primary only)
  • If rescission rights not disclosed: extended to 3 years
  • TRID tolerance cure: within 3 calendar years of closing
  • ECOA adverse action: within 30 days of completed application
  • ECOA record retention: 25 months (consumer) / 12 months (business)
FHA / VA / USDA Numbers
  • FHA min credit score (3.5% down): 580
  • FHA min credit score (10% down): 500

  • VA funding fee (subsequent use, 0% down): ~3.3%
  • VA disabled veterans: EXEMPT from funding fee
  • USDA income limit: 115% of area median income
Underwriting / Credit Numbers
  • Conventional PMI required: LTV above 80%
  • PMI auto-cancels: at 78% LTV (Homeowners Protection Act)
  • DU max DTI: 45% (50% with compensating factors)
  • QM manual underwrite max DTI: 43%
  • QM points & fees cap: 3% of loan amount
  • Seasoned funds: in account at least 60 days
  • SS income gross-up: divide by 0.75 (adds ~25%)
  • Rental income: use 75% of gross rents
RESPA Numbers
  • Escrow cushion max: 2 months of projected payments
  • RESPA Section 8 civil penalty: 3x the charge paid
  • Section 8 criminal penalty: up to $10,000 fine + 1 year imprisonment
  • RESPA Section 9: seller cannot require specific title company
  • QWR acknowledgment: within 5 business days
  • QWR substantive response: within 30 business days (extendable to 45)
Ethics / Fraud Numbers
  • SAR filing deadline: 30 days from detecting suspicious activity
  • SAR extension: up to 60 days if suspect ID needed
  • Texas homestead equity max LTV: 80%
  • Texas home equity fee cap: 3%
  • Texas home equity waiting period: 12 days after application
  • Texas foreclosure notice: 21 days
  • Texas foreclosure sale: first Tuesday of each month
