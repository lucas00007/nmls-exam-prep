Case Briefing START HERE

ECOA — the Equal Credit Opportunity Act — prohibits discrimination in any aspect of a credit transaction. Implemented by Regulation B, enforced by the CFPB. It covers lending from application through closing and beyond.

EXHIBIT A
What Is ECOA?
Equal Credit Opportunity Act. Implemented by Regulation B. Enforced by the CFPB. Prohibits discrimination in any aspect of a credit transaction based on protected classes.
Foundation
Exam note: ECOA covers ALL aspects of a credit transaction — not just the final approval/denial. Application processing, pricing, terms offered, and service quality are all covered. Even discouraging someone from applying can be a violation.

EXHIBIT B
ECOA Protected Classes
Race · Color · Religion · National Origin · Sex · Marital Status · Age · Receipt of Public Assistance. Note: Familial Status and Disability are FHA-only, not ECOA.
Memorize these
Exam trap: Marital status and age are ECOA-protected but NOT Fair Housing Act protected. Familial status and disability are FHA-protected but NOT ECOA (well, disability has limited ECOA coverage). Know which law covers which class.
▼ tap to expand exam note
EXHIBIT C
Adverse Action Timing
After a COMPLETE application, lender must provide adverse action notice within 30 days. The notice must include specific, actionable reasons — not vague conclusions.
Key deadline
Exam trap: "Insufficient creditworthiness" is NOT a specific reason under Reg B. Specific reasons: "credit score below minimum 620" or "debt-to-income ratio exceeds 45%." Vague reasons violate ECOA even if the timing is correct.

FILE 001
Adverse Action Notice Content
Must include: specific reason(s) for denial · ECOA notification statement · FCRA credit bureau disclosure (if credit report used) · Right to obtain free report within 60 days.
Content required
Exam note: ECOA and FCRA both apply to credit denials. ECOA requires specific denial reasons. FCRA requires identifying the credit bureau used and the borrower's right to a free report. Both sets of disclosures must be included.

FILE 002
Record Retention
ECOA Regulation B requires lenders to retain credit application records for 25 months from the date of the credit decision notification.
25 months
Exam trap: This is 25 months — not 12, not 36, not 7 years. From the date of notification of the credit DECISION — not the application date. 25 months allows regulators to review patterns for fair lending compliance.

FILE 003
Discouragement Prohibition
ECOA prohibits discouraging applicants from applying based on protected characteristics. "I don't think you'd qualify" said to only certain groups = ECOA violation even with no formal application.
No discouragement
Exam note: Discouragement is subtle and frequently tested. Telling one group "our rates are very high right now" while giving another group normal sales service = potential ECOA violation. The standard applies to ALL aspects of the credit process, including initial contact.

FILE 004
Spousal Signature Rules
Under ECOA, a lender CANNOT require a spouse's signature on a loan if the applicant individually qualifies. Requiring spousal co-signing for married borrowers but not single borrowers = ECOA violation.
Spousal rights
Exception: A spouse's signature may be required on documents that establish the security interest in jointly-owned property (e.g., deed of trust in community property states). The key is: you cannot require spousal income to qualify when the applicant qualifies alone.
▼ tap to expand exam note
FILE 005
Income from Public Assistance
Lenders CANNOT discount or refuse to count income from public assistance programs (Social Security, disability, welfare) when evaluating creditworthiness. Receipt of public assistance is a protected class.
Protected income
Exam note: This trips up many candidates. You cannot treat public assistance income as less reliable or less valuable than earned income. If the income is documented and stable, it must be counted at full value — same as any other income source.

FILE 006
Age Discrimination Rules
Age is an ECOA protected class. Cannot deny credit solely because of age. EXCEPTION: lenders may consider age in a statistically sound credit scoring system, or when it is relevant to a government loan program.
Age rules
Exception detail: A statistically validated credit scoring system may use age as a factor IF it is actuarially justified and does not discriminate against older applicants. This is a narrow exception — general age discrimination remains prohibited.

FILE 007
Disparate Treatment vs. Disparate Impact
Disparate treatment = intentionally treating similarly situated borrowers differently based on protected class. Disparate impact = neutral policy with disproportionate adverse effect on a protected class.
Two theories
Key difference: Disparate treatment requires discriminatory intent. Disparate impact does NOT require intent — if a neutral policy disproportionately harms a protected class, it may violate ECOA even if unintentional. Both are recognized as ECOA violations.

FILE 008
Incomplete Applications
Lender must notify the applicant of incompleteness within 30 days. The notice must specify what information is needed. Lender cannot simply sit on an incomplete application indefinitely.
30 days
Exam note: If a lender receives an incomplete application, they have two options: (1) deny it and send adverse action notice, or (2) send an incompleteness notice specifying what is needed within 30 days. Ignoring it is not an option.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

  • Regulation B implements ECOA
  • 8 protected classes including marital status and age
  • Adverse action: 30 days from complete application
  • Specific denial reasons required — vague is insufficient
  • Record retention: 25 months from decision notification
  • Discouragement alone can be a violation

EXAM TRAPS

  • Marital status = ECOA only (not FHA)
  • Familial status = FHA only (not ECOA)
  • 25 months retention — not 12 or 36
  • Discounting public assistance income = violation
  • Spousal co-sign not required if applicant qualifies
  • Vague denial reasons = ECOA violation
