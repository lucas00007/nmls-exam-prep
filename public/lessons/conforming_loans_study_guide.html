What Is a Conforming Loan?

A conforming loan is a conventional mortgage that meets the purchase guidelines established by Fannie Mae (FNMA) and Freddie Mac (FHLMC) — the two government-sponsored enterprises (GSEs) that dominate the U.S. secondary mortgage market. "Conforming" means the loan conforms to their standards in two key ways: loan amount and underwriting guidelines.

Why "Conforming" Matters
When a loan conforms to GSE standards, the lender can sell it to Fannie or Freddie on the secondary market. This replenishes the lender's capital to make new loans. Conforming = eligible for GSE purchase.
Two Requirements to Conform
1. Loan amount must be at or below the conforming loan limit

2. Underwriting must meet Fannie/Freddie guidelines (credit, DTI, LTV, documentation)
Who Sets the Rules?
The Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA) regulates Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac and sets the conforming loan limits each year based on changes in average U.S. home prices.
⚡ Critical Distinction

Conventional loan = any mortgage NOT backed by a government agency (not FHA, VA, or USDA).

Conforming loan = a conventional loan that ALSO meets Fannie/Freddie purchase guidelines.

All conforming loans are conventional — but not all conventional loans are conforming. A jumbo loan is conventional but NOT conforming because it exceeds the loan limit.

🏔️ High-Cost Area Limits

In designated high-cost areas (like Hawaii, Alaska, Guam, U.S. Virgin Islands, and certain expensive metro areas), the limit is 150% of the baseline:

  • 1-Unit: $1,149,825
  • 2-Unit: $1,472,250
  • 3-Unit: $1,779,525
  • 4-Unit: $2,211,600
📅 How Limits Are Set
  • FHFA reviews limits every October/November
  • New limits take effect January 1 each year
  • Based on FHFA House Price Index — if home prices rise, limits rise
  • Limits have increased significantly since 2020 due to rapid home price appreciation
  • Historical baseline: $417,000 (2006–2016), $424,100 (2017)
🎯
Exam Trap — Loan Amount vs. Purchase Price

The conforming loan limit applies to the LOAN AMOUNT — not the purchase price. A borrower can purchase a $900,000 home with a conforming loan if they put enough down. Example: $900,000 home × 20% down = $720,000 loan — this is BELOW the $766,550 limit and qualifies as conforming.

03 🏛️

Fannie Mae vs. Freddie Mac

Both Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac are Government-Sponsored Enterprises (GSEs) placed into federal conservatorship by FHFA in 2008. They do not make loans directly — they buy loans from lenders and package them into mortgage-backed securities (MBS).

Feature Fannie Mae (FNMA) Freddie Mac (FHLMC)
Full Name Federal National Mortgage Association Federal Home Loan Mortgage Corporation
Founded 1938 (New Deal era) 1970 (to create competition)
Regulator FHFA (since 2008 conservatorship) FHFA (since 2008 conservatorship)
AUS System Desktop Underwriter (DU) Loan Product Advisor (LPA) (formerly Loan Prospector)
Buys From Primarily mortgage banks and larger lenders Primarily savings institutions and banks
Loan Limits Same as Freddie Mac (set by FHFA) Same as Fannie Mae (set by FHFA)
3% Down Program HomeReady® Home Possible®
What They Do Neither makes loans directly. Both buy loans, package as MBS, and sell to investors — providing liquidity to the mortgage market.
💡 Conservatorship — What It Means

In September 2008, the federal government placed both GSEs into conservatorship under FHFA. This means the government effectively took control to stabilize them during the financial crisis. They are still in conservatorship today. Their debt is considered to have an implicit government backing — not an explicit guarantee like FHA loans, but investors treat GSE-backed securities as very low risk.

To be eligible for purchase by Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, a loan must meet specific underwriting requirements. These are the most commonly tested standards on the NMLS exam.

🤖 Automated Underwriting Systems (AUS)

Most conforming loans go through an AUS before human review:

  • Desktop Underwriter (DU) — Fannie Mae's AUS. Provides an "Approve/Eligible" finding for compliant loans.
  • Loan Product Advisor (LPA) — Freddie Mac's AUS. Provides an "Accept" finding for compliant loans.
  • AUS findings do NOT guarantee the loan will be purchased — lenders still verify all information in the file.
  • Manual underwriting is required when AUS cannot provide a decision or returns a "Refer" finding.
  • Caution finding — AUS flags a potential problem; requires underwriter review.
Asset Requirements
Reserves: Funds remaining after closing. Generally 2 months PITI for primary residence, more for investment properties.

Acceptable assets: Checking, savings, retirement accounts (60–70% of vested value), stocks and bonds

Gift funds: Allowed for primary residences. Must document donor, relationship, transfer, and that no repayment is expected.
Property Requirements
1-4 unit residential only

Condition: Must be habitable, safe, and sound at closing

Appraisal: Required on most transactions. Appraiser must be licensed, independent, and use URAR form (Fannie 1004)

Condos: Must be on Fannie/Freddie approved condo list or undergo project review

Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI)

PMI protects the lender (not the borrower) against loss if the borrower defaults. It is required on conventional loans when the LTV exceeds 80%. PMI is unique to conventional loans — FHA has MIP, VA has no MI, and USDA has guarantee fees.

LTV > 80%
Less than 20% down payment at origination
COST

Annual premium based on credit score, LTV, and loan type
PAYMENT
Monthly
Added to P&I + taxes + insurance escrow
CANCEL
78% LTV
Automatic cancellation per Homeowners Protection Act
PMI Cancellation Method How It Works Requirement
Automatic Cancellation PMI is automatically cancelled when LTV reaches 78% based on the original amortization schedule Loan must be current; no action needed from borrower
Borrower Request at 80% Borrower can REQUEST cancellation when LTV reaches 80% based on original value Good payment history required; lender may require appraisal
New Appraisal — Appreciation If home value has increased, borrower can request cancellation based on NEW appraised value Typically requires 2+ years of ownership and LTV below 75–80% on new value
Automatic at Midpoint PMI must be cancelled at the midpoint of the amortization schedule Even if LTV hasn't reached 78% — required by HPA
🏛️ Homeowners Protection Act (HPA)
  • Federal law governing PMI cancellation rights
  • Requires lenders to notify borrowers of PMI cancellation rights at closing
  • Automatic cancellation at 78% LTV (original value, original schedule)
  • Mandatory cancellation at loan midpoint
  • Annual statements required showing when PMI will be cancelled
  • Does NOT apply to FHA MIP (different rules apply)
💡 PMI vs. MIP vs. Guarantee Fee
  • PMI — Conventional loans, cancellable, protects lender
  • MIP — FHA loans, upfront + annual, most loans for life of loan if LTV started above 90%
  • VA Funding Fee — One-time upfront fee (2.15% first use), no monthly MI
  • USDA Guarantee Fee — Upfront (1%) + annual (0.35%), can be financed
🎯
Exam Trap — PMI Cancellation Numbers

The exam tests 80% vs. 78% constantly. Remember: borrower can REQUEST at 80% but PMI is automatically CANCELLED at 78%. These are two different numbers and two different events. Also: the 78% is based on the ORIGINAL value and ORIGINAL amortization schedule — not the current appraised value or actual payment history.

🔑 FHA MIP Rules — Heavily Tested
  • All FHA loans: Upfront MIP = 1.75% of base loan amount (paid at closing or financed into loan)
  • Annual MIP: ~0.55% for most 30-year loans (paid monthly)
  • If original LTV > 90%: Annual MIP for the LIFE OF THE LOAN — cannot be cancelled
  • If original LTV ≤ 90%: Annual MIP cancels after 11 years
  • To eliminate MIP: borrower must refinance into a conventional loan once they have 20% equity
  • The only way to remove FHA MIP on most loans is to refinance — it does NOT cancel automatically like PMI
07 🔄

The Secondary Mortgage Market

Understanding the secondary market is essential — it explains WHY conforming standards exist and WHY lenders care about them. The entire system is designed to move capital efficiently from investors to homebuyers.

STEP 1
Borrower Applies
Lender originates loan using its own capital or warehouse line of credit
STEP 2
Loan Closes
Lender funds loan, holds it temporarily on its balance sheet
STEP 3
Sold to GSE
Lender sells conforming loan to Fannie or Freddie, recovers capital
STEP 4
Pooled as MBS
GSE pools thousands of loans, issues Mortgage-Backed Securities
STEP 5
Sold to Investors
Pension funds, insurers, foreign governments buy MBS — capital flows back
Primary Market
Where loans are originated — between lenders and borrowers. MLOs operate in the primary market. The transaction is between the mortgage company and the homebuyer.
Secondary Market
Where loans are bought and sold after origination — between lenders, GSEs, and investors. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac operate in the secondary market. MBS are traded here.
Why It Matters for Rates
When investors want more MBS, they buy more, pushing yields down — which lowers mortgage rates. When investors sell, yields rise — rates increase. The secondary market is why 10-year Treasury yield is a key mortgage rate indicator.
💡 Mortgage-Backed Securities (MBS) Explained

An MBS is a bond backed by a pool of mortgages. When you make your monthly mortgage payment, that money flows through to the MBS investors. Fannie and Freddie guarantee the timely payment of principal and interest on their MBS — so even if borrowers default, investors still get paid (which is why these securities have low risk and strong investor demand).

🎯
Exam Question Type — Primary vs. Secondary Market

The exam frequently asks: "Where does an MLO originate loans?" Answer: Primary market. "Where does Fannie Mae operate?" Answer: Secondary market. These are consistently tested and students frequently mix them up.

08 🏔️

Non-Conforming & Jumbo Loans

When a conventional loan does NOT meet conforming standards, it becomes a non-conforming loan. The most common reason is exceeding the loan limit — these are called jumbo loans.

📋 Reasons a Loan Is Non-Conforming
  • Loan amount exceeds conforming limit — most common (jumbo)
  • Credit score below minimum
  • DTI ratio too high
  • Non-warrantable condo (not on approved list)
  • Property condition issues
  • Alternative income documentation (bank statement loans, DSCR loans)
  • Negative amortization or other prohibited features
🦁 Jumbo Loan Characteristics
  • Loan amount exceeds FHFA conforming limit
  • Cannot be sold to Fannie or Freddie
  • Lenders hold in portfolio OR sell to private secondary market
  • Typically requires higher credit score (720–740+)
  • May require larger down payment (10–20%+)
  • Often have different rate and term options
  • Stricter reserve requirements (12+ months common)
  • Rates may be higher OR lower than conforming (varies by market)
Portfolio Loans
Lenders who originate non-conforming loans often keep them "in portfolio" — on their own balance sheet rather than selling them. Portfolio lenders have more flexibility to set their own guidelines but bear all the credit risk themselves.
Non-QM Loans
Loans that don't meet the QM rule requirements. These are non-conforming by definition. Examples: bank statement loans (self-employed income), DSCR loans (rental income only), asset depletion loans. Higher risk, higher rates, no QM safe harbor for lender.
Super-Conforming / Agency High Balance
In designated high-cost areas, loans between the standard limit ($766,550) and the high-cost limit ($1,149,825) are called "super-conforming" or "high-balance conforming." These CAN be sold to Fannie/Freddie but may have slightly stricter guidelines.

10 🎯

Exam Traps & High-Frequency Topics

These are the most common ways the NMLS exam tries to trick candidates on conforming loan questions. Know these cold.

🪤 Trap #1 — Conforming vs. Conventional
All conforming loans are conventional, but not all conventional loans are conforming. A jumbo loan is conventional but NOT conforming. When the exam says "conventional" it may mean any non-government loan including jumbo. When it says "conforming" it specifically means within GSE limits and guidelines.
🪤 Trap #2 — Loan Amount vs. Purchase Price
The loan amount must be at or below the conforming limit — not the purchase price. A borrower can buy a $1M home with a conforming loan if their down payment brings the loan amount below the limit. Always calculate the actual loan amount.
🪤 Trap #3 — PMI 80% vs. 78%
Borrower can request PMI cancellation at 80%. PMI is automatically cancelled at 78%. Two different events, two different numbers. The 78% uses the ORIGINAL purchase price and ORIGINAL amortization schedule — not current appraised value or actual extra payments made.
🪤 Trap #4 — FHA MIP vs. PMI
FHA MIP does NOT cancel automatically on most loans. PMI on conventional loans DOES cancel. If a borrower on an FHA loan wants to eliminate MIP after gaining equity, they must refinance into a conventional loan — they cannot just request MIP cancellation like PMI.
🪤 Trap #5 — Primary vs. Secondary Market
MLOs originate loans in the primary market. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac operate in the secondary market. They do NOT directly lend to borrowers. They buy already-closed loans from lenders. This distinction is tested constantly.
🪤 Trap #6 — DU vs. LPA
DU (Desktop Underwriter) = Fannie Mae. LPA (Loan Product Advisor) = Freddie Mac. An AUS approval is NOT a loan approval — it is a finding indicating eligibility for purchase if the loan file matches what was entered. Lenders still verify all data.
🪤 Trap #7 — Self-Employed Threshold
A borrower is considered self-employed if they own 25% or more of a business. This triggers the requirement for 2 years of business AND personal tax returns. A 24% owner is NOT self-employed for qualifying purposes.
🪤 Trap #8 — Who PMI Protects
PMI protects the LENDER, not the borrower. It pays the lender if the borrower defaults. Yet the borrower pays the premiums. The exam loves asking "who does PMI protect?" — the answer is always the lender.
✅ Quick Reference — Fannie/Freddie Special Programs
  • Fannie Mae HomeReady® — 3% down, 97% LTV, 620+ credit, income must be ≤ 80% of Area Median Income (AMI), reduced PMI rates, allows non-borrower household income to be counted for qualifying purposes
  • Freddie Mac Home Possible® — 3% down, 97% LTV, 660+ credit score for most scenarios, income must be ≤ 80% AMI, reduced PMI rates, similar structure to HomeReady
  • Standard 5% down conventional — Does not have income limits, available to any qualified borrower
  • Both programs — Require homebuyer education course for first-time buyers
⚡ Last-Minute Memory Anchors
  • Conforming = meets Fannie/Freddie guidelines in BOTH loan amount AND underwriting
  • FHFA regulates Fannie and Freddie and sets annual loan limits
  • DU = Desktop Underwriter = Fannie Mae
  • LPA = Loan Product Advisor = Freddie Mac
  • PMI cancels at 78%, borrower requests at 80%, midpoint mandatory — all original values
  • FHA MIP doesn't cancel on most loans — must refinance to conventional to eliminate it
  • VA = 0% down, no PMI, funding fee instead, veterans/active duty only
  • USDA = 0% down, rural areas, income limits apply, upfront + annual fees
  • Primary market = where loans are made. Secondary market = where loans are sold
  • 3% down (conventional) requires HomeReady or Home Possible — has income limits
