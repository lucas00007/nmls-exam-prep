Case Briefing START HERE

Texas has some of the most unique mortgage laws in the country — written directly into the state constitution. Texas 50(a)(6) home equity rules, non-judicial foreclosure procedures, and TDSML licensing requirements are all tested on the Texas-specific portion of the NMLS exam.

EXHIBIT A
Texas Section 50(a)(6)
Texas Constitution limits home equity lending: max 80% CLTV, 3% fee cap, 12-day waiting period, closing at lender/attorney/title office only, no prepayment penalty, one loan at a time.
Constitutional rule
Exam trap: These rules are IN THE TEXAS CONSTITUTION — not just a statute. This makes them extremely powerful. If a lender violates 50(a)(6) and fails to cure within 60 days of written notice, the borrower can seek FORFEITURE of all principal and interest. That is the nuclear remedy.

EXHIBIT B
Texas Foreclosure — First Tuesday
Non-judicial (trustee) foreclosure sales occur on the first Tuesday of each month, 10 AM–4 PM, at the county courthouse. 21-day advance written notice required. Exception: if first Tuesday is Jan 1 or July 4 → moves to WEDNESDAY.
Most tested TX fact
The two exceptions to remember: January 1 (New Year's Day) and July 4 (Independence Day) — if the first Tuesday falls on either, the sale moves to the following WEDNESDAY. Only these two holidays trigger the Wednesday exception. No other holidays move the sale.

EXHIBIT C
TDSML — Texas Regulator
Texas Department of Savings and Mortgage Lending regulates non-bank mortgage companies and MLOs. Maximum penalty: $25,000 per violation per day. Pre-licensing: 23 hours. Annual CE: 8 hours.
State regulator
Exam note: Texas requires 23 pre-licensing hours (20 federal + 3 Texas-specific) and 8 annual CE hours (7 federal + 1 Texas-specific). The $25,000/violation/day penalty is one of the highest in the nation and reflects Texas's strong enforcement posture.

FILE 001
80% CLTV Cap — Math
Maximum CLTV on any Texas home equity transaction = 80% of fair market value. All liens combined (first mortgage + HELOC + HEL) cannot exceed 80%. This is a hard constitutional cap.
Hard cap
Calculation: Home worth $500,000 × 80% = $400,000 max total liens. If first mortgage balance = $380,000, max HEL = $400,000 - $380,000 = $20,000. If first mortgage = $400,000 exactly, max HEL = $0 — no borrowable equity under Texas rules.

FILE 002
3% Fee Cap
Total fees on a Texas 50(a)(6) home equity loan cannot exceed 3% of the original principal amount. Violation remedy: lender has 60 days after written notice to cure. Failure to cure = potential forfeiture of all P&I.
Fee limit
Cure process: If fees exceeded 3%, the borrower must send written notice to the lender. The lender then has 60 days to cure (refund the excess). If the lender fails to cure within 60 days, the borrower may bring an action for forfeiture of ALL principal and interest. Powerful remedy.

FILE 003
12-Day Waiting Period
A borrower cannot close a Texas home equity loan until at least 12 calendar days after submitting the written loan application. This is a mandatory constitutional cooling-off period.
Calendar days
Exam trap: This is 12 CALENDAR days — not business days. And it is from the date the owner submits the WRITTEN application — not from any oral inquiry. This 12-day wait is IN ADDITION to the standard TRID CD 3-business-day waiting period. Both must be satisfied.

FILE 004
Where Closing Must Occur
Texas home equity loan closings MUST occur at: (1) lender's permanent office · (2) attorney's office · (3) title company. Cannot close at the borrower's home, workplace, hospital, or any other location.
Location restriction
Purpose: This rule prevents high-pressure in-home closings where borrowers feel coerced. By requiring closing at a neutral professional location, Texas ensures the borrower is in a formal setting where they can properly review documents. Closing anywhere else = constitutional violation.

FILE 005
One Loan at a Time
A Texas homestead can have only ONE 50(a)(6) home equity loan at a time. Cannot have a first mortgage and a HELOC simultaneously if the HELOC is a 50(a)(6) loan. Must pay off and release existing equity lien first.
One at a time
Practical implication: If a homeowner wants to refinance their 50(a)(6) HELOC and take out a new home equity loan, they must first pay off and have the existing HELOC released, then wait, then originate the new loan. They cannot have two equity liens on the same Texas homestead simultaneously.

FILE 006
Texas Foreclosure Notice
Lender must send written notice at least 21 days before the foreclosure sale date. Notice must be: (1) sent by certified mail to last known address AND (2) posted at the courthouse.
21 days
Exam note: Both delivery methods are required simultaneously — certified mail AND courthouse posting. The 21-day count runs from the date of the NOTICE to the date of the SALE, not from the date of default. Texas non-judicial foreclosure can proceed very quickly once notice is properly given.

FILE 007
Texas Pre-Licensing Hours
Texas requires 23 total pre-licensing hours: 20 federal hours (required by SAFE Act) + 3 Texas-specific hours covering Texas laws, TDSML regulations, and Texas-specific practices.
23 hours total
Annual CE breakdown: 8 total hours per year: 3 hours federal law, 2 hours ethics, 2 hours non-traditional products, 1 hour Texas-specific. The 1 Texas-specific CE hour is above the 7-hour federal baseline. All CE must be from NMLS-approved providers before December 31 renewal deadline.

FILE 008
SAFE Act Mandatory Bars
Two permanent bars to MLO licensure: (1) ANY felony within preceding 7 years · (2) ANY felony EVER involving fraud, dishonesty, breach of trust, or money laundering. Category 2 has NO time limit.
Permanent bars
Exam trap: Category 2 (fraud/dishonesty felony) is PERMANENT — no time limit, no exceptions. A person convicted of mortgage fraud in 1990 is STILL permanently barred from MLO licensure today. This is among the most tested SAFE Act facts. Most candidates know the 7-year rule but miss the permanent category 2 bar.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

  • 50(a)(6): 80% CLTV cap — all liens combined
  • 50(a)(6): 3% fee cap — 60 days to cure violations
  • 50(a)(6): 12 calendar day waiting period
  • Foreclosure: first Tuesday, 10AM-4PM, 21 days notice
  • Jan 1 or July 4 first Tuesday → moves to Wednesday
  • SAFE Act: fraud felony bar is PERMANENT — no time limit

EXAM TRAPS

  • 12 calendar days wait — not business days
  • 80% CLTV includes ALL liens — first + second + HELOC
  • Closing at home = constitutional violation even if borrower requests
  • SAFE Act Category 2: fraud felony = permanent — no exceptions
  • First Tuesday only — not any Tuesday of the month
  • TDSML penalty = $25,000/violation/DAY
