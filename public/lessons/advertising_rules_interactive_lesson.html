Case Briefing START HERE

Advertising rules draw from TILA, RESPA, FHA, and the CFPB's UDAP authority. One wrong word in a mortgage ad can trigger federal violations across multiple laws. Master which terms trigger disclosures and what you can never say.

EXHIBIT A
Trigger Terms — TILA
Any specific rate, payment, loan amount, or down payment % in an ad triggers ALL 5 required TILA disclosures: APR · Down payment · Repayment terms · Payment amount · Number of payments.
Most tested
Exam trap: APR alone can be advertised without triggering anything extra. But a specific interest rate requires APR to appear with equal or greater prominence. "6.5% rate" = triggers everything. "Competitive rates" = safe. Specific vs. vague is the key distinction.

EXHIBIT B
NMLS # Required
All mortgage advertisements must include the MLO's NMLS ID number AND the company's NMLS ID number. This applies to websites, business cards, flyers, email signatures, and social media.
Always required
Exam note: NMLS numbers must appear on ALL advertisements — not just formal printed materials. A Facebook post offering mortgage rates, a text message about a refinance deal, and an email promoting loan programs all require NMLS numbers to be compliant.

EXHIBIT C
UDAP — Unfair & Deceptive
CFPB prohibits Unfair, Deceptive, or Abusive Acts or Practices. "Guaranteed lowest rate," false urgency, bait-and-switch tactics, and misrepresenting loan costs all violate UDAP standards.
CFPB enforcement
Three UDAP standards: UNFAIR = causes substantial injury consumers cannot avoid. DECEPTIVE = likely to mislead a reasonable consumer about material facts. ABUSIVE = takes unreasonable advantage of consumer vulnerability. Any one of the three is enough for a violation.

FILE 001
Safe vs. Trigger Language
SAFE: "Low rates available" · "Flexible terms" · "Call for today's rates" · "Low down payment options." TRIGGER: "5.5% rate" · "Only $1,299/month" · "$0 down" · "5% down payment."
Know the difference
The rule: Specific numbers trigger. Vague descriptors are safe. "As low as 5%" is still a specific number — it triggers. "Competitive rates" is vague — it does not trigger. When in doubt: if a consumer could rely on the specific number to make a financial decision, it is a trigger term.

FILE 002
ARM Advertising Rules
ARM ads must disclose that the rate may increase after the initial period. Cannot advertise only the low initial teaser rate without disclosing the adjustment feature clearly.
ARM-specific
Exam note: An ad showing "3.5% rate!" for a 5/1 ARM without disclosing that the rate adjusts after 5 years violates TILA advertising rules. The adjustable feature must be clearly disclosed — not hidden in fine print. "3.5% initial rate, then adjusts annually" is required.

FILE 003
FHA/VA Loan Advertising
Cannot imply government endorsement or affiliation. Cannot use official government logos or seals without authorization. "FHA approved lender" is fine. "United States Government Mortgage Program" is deceptive.
No false affiliation
Exam note: Private lenders can advertise FHA and VA loan products but CANNOT create the impression they are government agencies or that the loans are government-operated programs. Envelopes and mailers designed to look like official government correspondence are a major CFPB enforcement focus.

FILE 004
"No Closing Cost" Claims
Claiming "no closing costs" when costs are actually rolled into the rate or added to the loan balance is deceptive. The costs still exist — they're just paid differently. Must disclose how.
Deceptive claim
UDAP analysis: "No closing costs" is deceptive if costs are actually being rolled into a higher rate or added to the principal. A reasonable consumer would believe they are paying zero. The correct disclosure: "No out-of-pocket closing costs — costs are included in your rate" or similar.

FILE 005
APR Prominence Rule
When a specific interest rate is advertised, the APR must appear with equal or greater prominence. APR in 6-point footnote text next to a large headline rate violates the clear and conspicuous standard.
Equal prominence
Clear and conspicuous standard: The APR must be in a size and placement comparable to the stated rate. If the rate is in 24-point bold headline text, the APR cannot be in 8-point footnote text. Both must be readily visible and readable to a reasonable consumer.

FILE 006
False Urgency / Bait-and-Switch
Creating false urgency ("Your rate locks expire tonight!") or advertising one rate then switching to a worse one at application = UDAP abusive/deceptive practices enforced by the CFPB.
UDAP violations
VA loan mailers: The CFPB has taken major action against lenders sending mailers to veterans claiming VA benefits expire, rates are about to skyrocket, or that the letter is from a government agency. False urgency targeting veterans is a high-priority CFPB enforcement area.

FILE 007
Social Media Requirements
All social media advertising (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn posts about loan offers) must comply with the same TILA advertising rules as print ads — including NMLS numbers and trigger term disclosures.
Digital ads same rules
Exam note: Character limits on social media do not create exceptions to advertising rules. If a tweet or post uses a trigger term, ALL required disclosures must appear in that same post or in an immediately accessible link with full disclosures. The medium does not change the obligation.

FILE 008
Guaranteed Rate Claims
"Guaranteed lowest rate in Texas!" or "Best rate in America!" — unverifiable absolute guarantees are UDAP deceptive practices. They are likely to mislead a reasonable consumer about a material fact.
UDAP deceptive
General puffery vs. specific guarantees: "We offer great rates" = general puffery, generally acceptable. "Guaranteed lowest rate" = specific, verifiable-sounding claim that cannot actually be guaranteed = UDAP deceptive. The more specific and verifiable-sounding the claim, the higher the UDAP risk.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

  • Specific numbers trigger — vague language is safe
  • All 5 TILA disclosures must appear in same ad
  • NMLS numbers required on ALL advertising materials
  • ARM ads must disclose rate adjustment feature clearly
  • APR must be as prominent as any stated rate
  • UDAP: unfair, deceptive, or abusive = violation

EXAM TRAPS

  • Social media NOT exempt from TILA advertising rules
  • APR alone = safe. Specific rate = triggers everything
  • NMLS# on business cards, social media, everything
  • False VA urgency = major CFPB enforcement priority
  • No closing costs claim can be deceptive — explain how
  • Government-like appearance = false affiliation violation
