Case Briefing START HERE

Ethics and UDAP questions make up 16% of the NMLS exam. They test your ability to identify conflicts of interest, professional obligations, the LO Compensation Rule, and the CFPB's UDAP standards. The right answer is almost always: put the borrower's interest first.

EXHIBIT A
Core MLO Duty
MLOs owe a duty of good faith and fair dealing to borrowers. This means: no misrepresentation, no steering to benefit the MLO at the borrower's expense, honest disclosure of all material facts.
Foundation
Exam note: The duty of good faith applies even when it costs the MLO money. If a loan does not serve the borrower's best interest, the ethical MLO discloses this — even if it means losing the commission. Professional obligation comes before personal financial gain.

EXHIBIT B
LO Compensation Rule
MLOs cannot steer borrowers to loan products that do not serve their interests in order to earn higher compensation. Anti-steering: must present the borrower with a range of options based on their needs.
Reg Z rule
Dual compensation: An MLO cannot receive compensation from BOTH the lender AND the borrower on the same transaction. If the lender pays the MLO, the borrower cannot also pay the MLO (except for bona fide third-party costs). This prevents double-dipping that harms borrowers.
▼ tap to expand exam note
EXHIBIT C
UDAP — Three Standards
Unfair = causes substantial injury consumers cannot reasonably avoid. Deceptive = likely to mislead a reasonable consumer about material facts. Abusive = takes unreasonable advantage of consumer vulnerability or limited understanding.
CFPB authority
Exam note: Any ONE of the three (unfair, deceptive, OR abusive) is sufficient for a UDAP violation. The CFPB does not need to prove all three. The abusive standard is the newest — targeting practices that exploit consumers who cannot reasonably protect themselves.

FILE 001
Occupancy Fraud
Representing an investment property as a primary residence to get better rates = federal mortgage fraud under 18 U.S.C. § 1014. MLOs who knowingly process such loans are criminally liable — even if the borrower initiated the fraud.
Federal crime
Key principle: "I was just processing the loan" is not a defense when the MLO knowingly assists in fraud. The crime is the knowing participation — not who initiated it. An MLO who sees clear signs of occupancy fraud and proceeds is criminally exposed.

FILE 002
Net Tangible Benefit
Recommending a refinance with closing costs that take 8 years to recover to a borrower who plans to sell in 2 years violates the MLO's ethical duty. Must serve the borrower's stated financial goals.
Borrower first
Practical application: Calculate break-even before recommending a refinance. If break-even exceeds the borrower's planned hold period, the refinance does not serve their interest. Recommending it anyway to earn a commission is both unethical and potentially a UDAP violation.

FILE 003
Elder Financial Exploitation
Warning signs: unusual urgency, online-only investment contacts, significant equity at risk, confusion about terms. MLOs may have legal reporting obligations. Proceeding blindly with exploitation warning signs is unethical.
Vulnerable borrowers
Abusive UDAP standard: Taking unreasonable advantage of a person who cannot protect their own interests — such as an elderly borrower showing confusion — violates the CFPB's abusive acts standard. In many states, MLOs have mandatory reporting obligations for suspected elder financial exploitation.

FILE 004
Document Fraud Obligations
When an MLO suspects falsified income documents, they MUST escalate to compliance — not proceed. Processing a loan with suspected fraud = potential criminal liability regardless of who falsified the documents.
Report it
Self-protection is not enough: An MLO who "notes the concern in the file but proceeds" is NOT protected. Documenting suspicion while continuing to originate the fraudulent loan can still constitute knowing participation. The correct action is STOP and REPORT — not proceed with documentation.

FILE 005
Fair Lending in Service Quality
Providing materially different levels of service (response times, thoroughness, follow-up) to borrowers based on race, national origin, or other protected class = fair lending violation even if all loans ultimately close.
Service quality
Paired testing: Regulators use paired testing to detect service quality disparities. Two "testers" with identical profiles but different protected characteristics contact the same lender. Systematic differences in response quality reveal discrimination even when no loan is denied.

FILE 006
ABA Disclosure Ethics
Referring borrowers to a company in which the MLO has a financial interest without written ABA disclosure is both a RESPA violation AND an ethics violation. The borrower has the right to make informed choices.
Transparency
Ethical principle behind the rule: Conflicts of interest must be disclosed so consumers can make informed decisions. Even if the affiliated company provides good service at fair prices, the undisclosed financial relationship undermines consumer trust and distorts the referral. Disclosure protects the consumer's right to choose.

FILE 007
Anti-Steering Obligation
When a lender offers higher compensation for steering borrowers to Product B over Product A, and Product A better serves the borrower — the MLO must recommend Product A. The compensation structure cannot override the borrower's best interest.
LO Comp Rule
Practical rule: MLO compensation can vary by lender — but NOT by loan product from the same lender. Steering to a higher-rate product from the same lender to earn more = LO Compensation Rule violation. The borrower must receive the best available option from that lender, not the one that pays the MLO more.

FILE 008
License Maintenance Ethics
Originating loans with an expired license is unlicensed activity — a serious violation. Completing CE and renewing on time is a professional obligation, not optional. License expiration requires immediate cessation of origination activity.
License duty
Exam note: When an MLO's license expires, they must STOP originating IMMEDIATELY. They cannot originate "just one more loan" before renewing. Unlicensed origination exposes the MLO and their company to regulatory action, civil liability, and potential criminal charges.

SCENARIO 01 / 03 — LO COMPENSATION RULE

KEY TAKEAWAYS

  • Borrower's best interest always comes first
  • LO Comp Rule: cannot steer for higher pay
  • UDAP: unfair + deceptive + abusive — any one violates
  • Occupancy fraud = federal crime, even if borrower initiated
  • Net tangible benefit required before recommending refi
  • Expired license = stop immediately, no exceptions

EXAM TRAPS

  • LO Comp Rule is Reg Z — not RESPA Section 8
  • Dual compensation: cannot receive from both lender AND borrower
  • Document fraud: document + proceed = participation
  • UDAP abusive: targets consumer vulnerability exploitation
  • Elder exploitation warning signs require intervention
  • Reporting discrimination is the RIGHT action — not a violation
